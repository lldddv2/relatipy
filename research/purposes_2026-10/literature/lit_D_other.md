## Q1. Sgr A* flares / hot spots (orbit model, parameters, sampler, spin, super-Keplerian)
Sources: WebSearch snippets/summaries only (primary pages blocked). Journal/volume items marked [v?] are unverified.

- GRAVITY Collab. 2018, A&A 618, L10, arXiv:1810.12641, "Detection of orbital motions near the last stable circular orbit of the massive black hole SgrA*". Model (per secondary summaries): circular Keplerian hot-spot orbit with light bending (ray-traced image); best fit ~7 r_g, i ~160° [UNVERIFIED]. Press-reported orbital speed ~0.3c [UNVERIFIED]. Spin: not constrained in retrieved text. Sampler: not retrieved.
- Bauböck et al. (GRAVITY 2020), A&A 635, A143 [v? volume from snippet, article no. from memory], arXiv:2002.08374, "Modeling the orbital motion of Sgr A*'s near-infrared flares". Three 2018 flares; Kerr-metric hot-spot orbits; out-of-plane motion and orbital shear included. Best fit (two summaries agree): hot spot at ~9 r_g, i ~140°, emitting region < ~5 r_g. Reduced chi^2 ~1.6 (Jul 22), ~4.5 (Jul 28), ~2.3 (May 27) — secondhand, from a Shahzadi-type paper [UNVERIFIED]. Spin/sampler: not in retrieved text.
- Matsumoto, Chan & PIRAN (NOT Psaltis) 2020, "The origin of hotspots around Sgr A*: Orbital or pattern motion?", arXiv:2004.13029, MNRAS (DOI 10.1093/mnras/staa2095) [volume unverified]. Fluid-tracking hot spots (circular Keplerian with spin; RIAF streamline) undershoot the observed Jul-22-2018 loop; circular Keplerian fit to partial loop at ~7–7.5 r_g. Authors favour pattern motion; ASJ 2020 abstract says a marginally bound geodesic has the same shortfall. Super-Keplerian: one summary says Matsumoto et al. found a circular orbit at ~2.7x Keplerian frequency, r = 12.5M, fits better [UNVERIFIED, conflicts with other summary]; arXiv:2405.10115 says Matsumoto et al. got a better fit with faster motion and shifted plane at cost of goodness of fit.
- Ball, Özel, Christian, Chan & Psaltis 2021, ApJ 917, 8, arXiv:2005.14251, "A Plasmoid Model for the Sgr A* Flares Observed with GRAVITY and Chandra". Non-geodesic plasmoid blobs on conical helices, 5 starting parameters (radius, 2 angles, 2 velocity components); GR light curves with light-travel delays and synchrotron cooling. Example starts r = 36 and 50 (units not given in excerpt). Spin/inclination/sampler: not in retrieved text [UNVERIFIED].
- Vos, Mościbrodzka & Wielgus 2022, "Polarimetric signatures of hot spots in black hole accretion flows", arXiv:2209.09931, A&A (2022, aa44840-22) [volume unverified]. Spot in orbit; mm polarimetric loops; a 30° inclination model reproduces the 230 GHz flux [UNVERIFIED].
- Wielgus et al. 2022, A&A 665, L6, DOI 10.1051/0004-6361/202244493, arXiv:2209.09926, "Orbital motion near Sagittarius A*. Constraints from polarimetric ALMA observations". 230 GHz polarimetry from April 2017 (after Apr 11 Chandra flare). Gaussian compact spot on equatorial orbit, ipole ray tracing [UNVERIFIED]. Loop metrics quoted in arXiv:2606.18107 (2026): asymmetry A ~1.3±0.1, time ratio R_t ~6.5±0.5, area ratio R_a ~550±50 [UNVERIFIED as to which paper].
- Yfantis, Mościbrodzka, Wielgus, Vos & Jimenez-Rosales 2024, A&A (aa48230-23), arXiv:2310.07762, "Fitting the light curves of Sgr A* with a hot-spot model: Bayesian modelling of QU loops in the millimetre band". Semi-analytic magnetized blob, ipole ray tracing, SAMPLER = dynesty (nested sampling) [summary]. Spin estimates a* > 0.8 across model set, but spin has smaller effect on light curves than other parameters [summary; UNVERIFIED].
- Yfantis et al. 2024 (companion), A&A 691, A327 (2024) [summary], arXiv:2408.07120, "Hot-spots around Sagittarius A*: Joint fits to astrometry and polarimetry". GRAVITY NIR astrometry + polarimetry, ipole. Best fit: i ~155°, period ~70 min, orbit ~12 r_g at ~1.3x Keplerian speed (SUPER-KEPLERIAN), spot < ~5 r_g; spin weakly determined [summary].
- GRAVITY Collab. 2023, A&A 677, L10 [summary], DOI 10.1051/0004-6361/202347416, arXiv:2307.11821, "Polarimetry and Astrometry of NIR Flares as Event Horizon Scale, Dynamical Probes for the Mass of Sgr A*". 4 flares astrometry, 6 polarimetry (2 overlap). Clockwise loops ~1 h; polarization period matches; flare radius ~9 r_g consistent with Keplerian orbits near ISCO; mass cross-check ~4.3e6 Msun [summary; UNVERIFIED]. No super-Keplerian claim in summary.
- Parameter study, arXiv:2405.10115, A&A DOI 10.1051/0004-6361/202450571 (2024), "Parameter study for hot spot trajectories around Sgr A*". Tests GRAVITY 2018a data; spin changes orbit shape little and fit quality changes gradually with spin; later sections assume a non-spinning hole [summary].
- Spin-inference inconsistency: inclinations 20–30° (some summaries) vs 140–160° (Bauböck 2020, Yfantis 2024, Matsumoto) are probably the same orbit family under i -> 180°−i, a sense-of-rotation convention difference [UNVERIFIED inference].
- Spin position angle: G23 reports 180°; a refit gives 130° ± 20°; Ball et al. 2021 quoted at 135° [ResearchGate table excerpt; UNVERIFIED].

Q1 takeaways (bias / bottleneck): (i) Model class drives the answer: circular Keplerian/light-bent (GRAVITY 2018, Matsumoto 2020) vs Kerr-geodesic hot spot (Bauböck 2020) vs non-geodesic helix (Ball 2021) vs semi-analytic blob with ipole (Yfantis 2024). (ii) Spin is weakly constrained by NIR astrometry (Yfantis 2024 joint; parameter study) but mm loops give a* > 0.8 (Yfantis 2024 dynesty) [summary]. (iii) Super-Keplerian claims (~1.3x; "faster motion") appear only in model-dependent fits; they may signal model misspecification rather than physics. (iv) Sampler: nested sampling (dynesty) is the one documented choice; GRAVITY sampler unknown.


## Q2. Pulsars near Sgr A* (timing model PN vs Kerr, forecasts, periods, PN truncation)
Sources: WebSearch snippets/summaries (primary pages blocked). [v?] = volume unverified; "secondhand" = summary of another paper.

- Liu, Wex, Kramer, Cordes & LAZIO (not Wharton) 2012, ApJ 747, 1 (ADS 2012ApJ...747....1L; DOI 10.1088/0004-637X/747/1/1), arXiv:1112.2151, "Prospects for Probing the Spacetime of Sgr A* with Pulsars". Method: conventional timing -> mass, spin, quadrupole with formal error covariance (Fisher-type), validated on mock pulsar data. Orbits shorter than ~0.3 yr assumed largely free of outside perturbation. Forecast over 5 yr, fast-spinning BH: ~1e-3 fractional for frame dragging, ~1e-2 for no-hair test (ADS and OSTI snippets agree). Timing precision needed ~100 microsec (SKA above 15 GHz). Timing model (PN vs exact Kerr): NOT retrieved, UNVERIFIED.
- Psaltis, Wex & Kramer 2016, ApJ 818, 121 (DOI 10.3847/0004-637X/818/2/121; published 16 Feb 2016), arXiv:1510.00394, "A Quantitative Test of the No-Hair Theorem with Sgr A* using stars, pulsars, and the EHT". Author list not directly confirmed in retrieved text (title + bibliography match). Numbers not retrieved. Secondhand: a 2020 A&A paper quotes ~1e-5 to 1e-3 precision for mass and spin attributed to Liu 2012 + Psaltis 2016 [secondhand]. Secondhand: Psaltis 2016 restricts pulsar analysis to periastron passages (where BH dominates) and treats pulsar errors as orthogonal to shadow-imaging errors [SKAO review, arXiv:2607.03078 summary]. Timing model: UNVERIFIED.
- Zhang & Saha 2017, ApJ 849, 33 (DOI 10.3847/1538-4357/aa8f47; published 27 Oct 2017), arXiv:1709.08341, "Probing the Spinning of the Massive Black Hole in the Galactic Center via Pulsar Timing: A Full Relativistic Treatment". Note: IOP lists it as ApJ 849, 33 (not ApJL 849 L33 as an earlier summary said). Model: full GR numerical orbit integration around Kerr (secondhand, citing papers). Spin-degeneracy: single-pulsar leading-order degeneracy broken either by astrometric proper motion or by a second pulsar in a differently tilted orbit (secondhand). Forecast (secondhand from arXiv:1709.08341 summary): ~8 yr of observing a normal pulsar on a ~2.6-yr orbit at 1-5 ms timing gives spin magnitude ~1e-3 to 1e-2 and orientation ~0.1-1 deg at 2 sigma; examples at sigma_T = 5 ms [UNVERIFIED].
- Hu, Shao & Zhang 2023 (Zexin Hu, Lijing Shao, Fupeng Zhang), PRD 108, 123034 (15 Dec 2023), arXiv:2312.01889, "Prospects for probing small-scale dark matter models with pulsars around Sgr A*". Timing model: post-Newtonian equations of motion, numerical (abstract level). Forecast: half-decade of weekly timing at 1 ms on an eccentric pulsar constrains the DM power-law index to ~20% (abstract level, from arXiv/INSPIRE/Kavli listing). Spin/quadrupole forecasts not retrieved.
- Hu & Shao 2024, arXiv:2408.00245, "Measuring the Spin of the Galactic Center Supermassive Black Hole with Two Pulsars". Venue: Physical Review Letters per a KIAA page [v?, UNVERIFIED]. Joint analysis of two or more pulsars; mock data: a second pulsar sharpens spin by ~2-3 orders of magnitude in some cases (exponent garbled in snippet; check). Argues that pulsars on wider orbits (P ~2-5 yr) are the likelier case. Single-pulsar benchmark quoted from it (secondhand): e >= 0.8, P_orb < ~0.5 yr, spin and quadrupole to ~1% in 5 yr at 1 ms timing [UNVERIFIED]. Proper-motion astrometry at ~10 microarcsec judged too coarse to help much [secondhand].
- Hu, Wang & Shao 2026, ApJS [v?] (DOI 10.3847/1538-4365/ae8d23), arXiv:2602.19546, "A Realistic Pulsar-Supermassive Black Hole Timing Model". TIMING MODEL = POST-NEWTONIAN: 2PN dynamics keeping leading-order spin-orbit and quadrupole terms; next-to-leading-order light-travel delays; Sgr A* proper motion included. Truncation assessment: not retrieved.
- Ben-Salem & Hackmann 2022, arXiv:2203.10931, "Relativistic propagation and frame dragging time delay in the timing of a pulsar orbiting the supermassive black hole SgrA*". Compares test-particle approximation with post-Newtonian expansion; states PN loses reliability on tight orbits (secondhand summary) [journal unverified]. This is the closest explicit truncation assessment found.
- Della Monica, De Martino & De Laurentis 2023, MNRAS 524, 3782 (DOI 10.1093/mnras/stad2125), arXiv:2305.18178. Numerical light-travel time in generic SPHERICALLY SYMMETRIC metrics (not Kerr); Schwarzschild test then three beyond-GR metrics; residual deviations spanning orders of magnitude; SKA detectability.
- SKAO review, arXiv:2607.03078 (2026, "Galactic Centre Pulsars with the SKAO"), secondhand: spin and quadrupole fractional precision ~1e-2 to 1e-3; flags that PN modelling may misrepresent a bright, tightly orbiting millisecond pulsar, where full GR is needed.
- arXiv:2609.07412 (2026), "Timing Gravity with Pulsars in the Strong Field": spherical-symmetry simplification; Kerr would add frame-dragging and quadrupole timing signatures omitted; for S2-like orbits pulsar mass constraints beat S-star astrometry by ~3 orders of magnitude (secondhand, UNVERIFIED).
- BlackHoleCam review, arXiv:1606.08879 (secondhand): mock data suggest quadrupole recoverable to ~1% or better for a pulsar on a few-month orbit, depending on spin and eccentricity.
- Early work: Pfahl & Loeb 2004, ApJ 615, 253 (ADS 2004ApJ...615..253P), "Probing the Spacetime around Sgr A* with Radio Pulsars" (authorship from memory for the second author).
- Discovery status: arXiv:2602.08922 (Breakthrough Listen / Perez et al. 2026), "On the Deepest Search for Galactic Center Pulsars and an Examination of an Intriguing Millisecond Pulsar Candidate": 8.19 ms MSP candidate, DM 2775 pc cm^-3, persistent over 1-h scan; separation from Sgr A* NOT established in retrieved text; no orbit; press says ApJ 9 Feb 2026 but arXiv record not confirmed [UNVERIFIED]. Also arXiv:2508.09931 (Shao & Hu, 2025 review) says no pulsar yet found in a tight (<~1 yr) orbit.

Q2 takeaways: (i) Timing models split: PN-based (Hu, Shao, Zhang 2023; Hu, Wang, Shao 2026) vs numerical Kerr geodesics (Zhang & Saha 2017, secondhand). PN truncation on tight orbits is flagged as a risk (Ben-Salem & Hackmann 2022; SKAO review 2026), not quantified in what I could retrieve. (ii) Forecasts span ~1e-5 to 1e-2 depending on timing precision (100 microsec to 5 ms), eccentricity, period (<0.3 yr vs 2.6 yr), span (5 vs 8 yr) and CL (2 sigma vs 95%). Comparison needs a common assumption set. (iii) Single-pulsar spin-orbit degeneracy means Fisher-type single-pulsar forecasts may be optimistic; two-pulsar or proper-motion breaks it (Hu & Shao 2024; Zhang & Saha 2017, secondhand).


## Q3. EMRIs: FastEMRIWaveforms, Chapman-Bird 2025, speed requirements, transient resonances, plunge
Sources: WebSearch snippets/summaries. NOTE: the WebSearch quota for this turn was exhausted after this question (tool refused further searches). Items marked "not searched" below were NOT covered.

- Katz, Chua, Speri, Warburton & Hughes 2021 (the 5th author is Scott A. HUGHES, not Barack, per arXiv byline in summary), PRD 104, 064047 (Sep 2021), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevD.104.064047 [FEW citation file, summary], arXiv:2104.04582. Title on arXiv: "FastEMRIWaveforms: New tools for millihertz gravitational-wave data analysis"; journal title: "Fast extreme-mass-ratio-inspiral waveforms: New tools for millihertz gravitational-wave data analysis". Content (summary): fully relativistic (eccentric) models for non-spinning holes; AAK-type approximate model for generic Kerr orbits. Speed figures from this paper: NOT retrieved (UNVERIFIED).
- Chapman-Bird et al. 2025, arXiv:2506.09470, "The Fast and the Frame-Dragging: Efficient waveforms for asymmetric-mass eccentric equatorial inspirals into rapidly-spinning black holes" (journal PRD per APS DOI 10.1103/scbp-75pf; volume unverified). Scope: eccentric EQUATORIAL Kerr orbits, spin magnitude up to a = 0.999; inclined Kerr orbits NOT covered by a fully relativistic model (summary). Generation time quoted near 100 ms "on accelerated hardware" (summary of abstract; no speed-up factor in excerpt). Another summary says FEW's fully relativistic Kerr equatorial waveforms with e < 0.9 take < 1 s (arXiv:2509.02394). Data release Zenodo 15631641; software DOI 10.5281/zenodo.15630565 (summary).
- FEW versions (summary): v2.0.0 (June 2025) faster and more accurate than v1.5.1, GPU TDI response via fastlisaresponse (cited in arXiv:2509.20062); v2.0 API has CPU_ONLY() amplitude-interpolation class; v2.1.0 limits starting eccentricity so evolutions stay inside the interpolation range.
- Per-evaluation cost, 2025: arXiv:2510.20891 ("Ab uno disce omnes: single-harmonic search"): ~100 ms per EMRI waveform evaluation; cost scales with harmonic count and number of in-band cycles; the paper avoids templates (summary).
- Population-scale cost, 2023: arXiv:2302.07043, "Computation of stochastic background from EMRI populations for LISA" (authors not retrieved): ~2 min per source; full procedure ~120 s per source; ~16 million sources for one model implies ~60 years on one CPU (summary). Mitigation: pre-screen with inclination-averaged AK truncated at the Schwarzschild LSO, then drop sources with SNR < 1 while tracking lost SNR.
- Accuracy vs cost (2023 summary of a waveform-family comparison): Teukolsky-based calculations need numerical integration plus a large mode sum; the numerical-kludge (NK) model agrees well with Teukolsky but is more expensive because it integrates the trajectory in both phase and coordinate space (summary; source paper not identified).
- Dark-siren cost-driven prior example: arXiv:2310.12813 (2023) fixed one prior bound for computational reasons (summary).
- Lead not confirmed: Singh, Chapman-Bird, Berry & Veitch 2025, "Constraints on the EMRI population from LISA data", arXiv:2508.16399 (title only seen in a reference list). NOT retrieved: its cost numbers.
- Arithmetic (mine, not a source): at ~100 ms per call, 1e6 likelihood evaluations ~ 28 CPU-hours; a 10x speed-up matters for population MCMC/Monte Carlo.
- Bottleneck assessment (mine, inference): in FEW-type architectures the trajectory and fluxes are precomputed/interpolated, so per-waveform cost is dominated by harmonic/mode sums and interpolation; the geodesic integration is NOT separately timed in anything I retrieved (UNVERIFIED). For generic inclined Kerr orbits no fully relativistic fast model was found, so the bottleneck is accuracy of the approximate (AAK-type) trajectory rather than speed.

Transient resonances:
- Flanagan & Hinderer 2012, "Transient resonances in the inspirals of point particles into black holes" (called PRL 2012 in one summary; volume unverified). Cited as ref. 46 in Berry et al. 2016 (summary).
- Ruangsri & Hughes (PN estimate of resonance duration) and Brink, Geyer & Hinderer (phase space of resonant Kerr orbits): both associated with arXiv:1311.4457 "Conditions for sustained orbital resonances in extreme mass ratio inspirals" (author attribution from summary; UNVERIFIED).
- Berry, Cole, Cañizares & Gair 2016, arXiv:1608.08951, "Importance of transient resonances in extreme-mass-ratio inspirals" (v1 31 Aug 2016; v2 Jan 2017 per summary). Adiabatic waveform models omit transient resonances; ~4% of detectable signals would be lost if resonances were omitted (summary). Phase error in radians: NOT found. Follow-up arXiv:1702.05481 "Understanding the importance of transient resonances in EMRIs" (same four authors, summary) repeats ~4% below rho = 15.
- Speri & Gair 2021, arXiv:2103.06306, "Assessing the impact of transient orbital resonances": 3:2 resonances can give parameter errors up to several times the statistical uncertainty for one-year inspirals (summary). Journal: UNVERIFIED.
- Levati & Cárdenas-Avendaño 2026, arXiv:2604.26011, "Parameter-estimation bias induced by transient orbital resonances in extreme-mass-ratio inspirals". Fisher-matrix analysis. For most orbits tested, omitting a resonance crossing loses SNR and biases parameters. The sign AND amplitude of the resonance-induced change to the integrals of motion must be modelled accurately (summary). Citing paper gives PRD 114(2), 024061 (2026) [summary; UNVERIFIED].
- Levati, Keeble & Cárdenas-Avendaño 2026, arXiv:2609.25392, "A Systematic Study of Resonance-Driven Flux Modifications in EMRIs" (summary; numbers not retrieved).
- PRD 104, 084011 (2021): scalar self-force analogue along r-theta-resonant geodesics (accepted manuscript; authors not retrieved).
- Not found: a "Speri 2024" transient-resonance paper (not in any retrieved text).
- Irrelevant hit: arXiv:2104.03422 "Importance of tidal resonances in EMRIs" (tidal, not Kerr).

Plunge modelling: NOT SEARCHED (quota exhausted). No plunge-specific source retrieved.

Q3 takeaways: (i) Fast Kerr support is equatorial-eccentric only (Chapman-Bird 2025); inclined Kerr uses AAK-type approximations, so model error may dominate speed as a concern. (ii) Speed scale ~0.1 s per waveform -> ~30-300 CPU-hours per 1e6-1e7 waveform evaluations at ~0.1 s each (own arithmetic, corrected). (iii) Transient resonances: SNR loss ~4% of population (Berry 2016) and parameter bias "several sigma" for 3:2 (Speri & Gair 2021); Levati 2026 says the sign and amplitude of the resonance kick must be right, i.e., a model-accuracy issue, not just speed.
